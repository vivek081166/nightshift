# Postmortem: sendgrid daily quota reached during a marketing send

Date: 2025-09-09  
Owner: Tom (notifier)  
Severity: P2  
Status: Closed (Review completed 2025-09-11)

---

## 1. Summary

On 2025-09-09 at 16:40 JST, our third-party email provider, sendgrid, began rejecting all outgoing emails with quota exceeded error responses. This occurred because a scheduled marketing email campaign was routed through the identical default sendgrid stream used for core transactional emails, including booking confirmations, customer receipts, and cancellation notices.

The sudden volume generated by the marketing send completely exhausted Sorrel's daily email sending quota with the provider. As a result, critical customer communications—predominantly appointment booking confirmations—were delayed for approximately three hours. 

Because `notifier` is architected to preserve message safety during provider-side outages or rejections, the worker nodes held all unacknowledged emails in the persistent queue and retried them safely. Once the daily quota window reset at 19:40 JST, delivery resumed automatically and the backlog cleared without message loss or permanent dropouts. All queued booking confirmations were successfully delivered by 20:05 JST. No customer data was exposed and no financial transactions were affected.

During the incident, the engineering team evaluated whether to request an immediate quota increase with the vendor. Raising the tier was formally rejected as an unnecessary purchasing decision by the service owner, Tom, given the vendor's reset timing and the architectural root cause. As a permanent remediation, marketing email was scheduled to be completely decoupled into an isolated stream with its own independent quota limit (completed on 2025-09-30).

---

## 2. Impact

- **Customer Experience:** Customers completing appointments between 16:40 JST and 19:40 JST experienced a delay of up to three hours in receiving booking confirmations, receipts, and appointment cancellation notices. Public web booking, slot reservations, and card processing via `payments` functioned normally throughout the window.
- **Support Volume:** Support received 47 customer inquiries regarding unreceived email confirmations during the three-hour window. Hana (support lead) coordinated responses confirming that bookings were successfully locked in the system and that confirmations were processing in a backlog.
- **Message Integrity:** 0 messages dropped. Zero duplicate sends recorded on the duplicate detection panel.
- **Data & Financial Exposure:** None. No customer records were leaked or misrouted. No money moved incorrectly.
- **SLO Impact:** Transactional confirmation delivery latency degraded significantly, but `api` and `web` request error budgets remained unaffected since `notifier` operates asynchronously.

---

## 3. Background & System Topology

Sorrel runs four core services: `web`, `api`, `payments`, and `notifier`. All customer notifications—receipts, reminders, and confirmations—originate asynchronously:

1. When a user creates or modifies an appointment, `api` commits the transaction to its regional database (e.g., in `ap-northeast`, our Tokyo hub) and enqueues a message payload onto `notifier`'s regional queue.
2. `notifier` operates three worker nodes per active region (`ap-northeast`, `us-east`, `eu-west`) alongside an off-peak scheduler. Workers pull from the queue, render templates, and call vendor APIs over HTTPS.
3. Outbound SMS messages are handed to `twilio` (primarily for one-time passcodes and appointment reminders). Outbound email messages are routed exclusively to `sendgrid`.

Under normal conditions, transactional email volume tracks booking rates steadily throughout the day, with modest spikes during morning transit (07:00–09:00 JST), lunchtime (12:00–13:00 JST), and evening checkout peaks (18:00–23:00 JST). Daily contracted provider allowances are sized to comfortably absorb standard operational spikes and off-peak batches.

However, Sorrel maintained a single production sending identity and stream on sendgrid. When promotional and seasonal marketing blasts were initiated, they were pushed directly to `notifier` without channel or account isolation, sharing the same egress pipeline and vendor quota as transactional receipts.

## 5. What Went Well

- **Queue Resilience and Safety:** `notifier`'s architecture performed strictly as designed. When sendgrid issued rejection codes, worker nodes did not drop messages or crash; instead, messages were retained on the queue and retried with backoff. Zero customer messages were lost.
- **Observability:** Metrics on the "Sorrel / notifier" dashboard made the failure signature obvious. Contrasting "delivered per minute" against "messages added per minute" clearly showed an egress vendor rejection rather than an ingress pipeline stall.
- **Log Tool Clarity:** Using structured logging with `service=notifier msg="quota"` surfaced the exact upstream vendor response within minutes, eliminating guesswork about whether we were dealing with network partitions or authentication failures.
- **Clear Owner Boundaries:** When the commercial decision of an emergency quota tier upgrade arose, on-call appropriately escalated to Tom rather than attempting manual account modifications outside standard permissions.

---

## 6. What Went Badly

- **Shared Provider Resource Limits:** Core transactional emails (which customers rely on immediately after spending money) were grouped into the same egress pool as non-urgent marketing emails. A high-volume marketing send was capable of starving critical business confirmations.
- **Lack of Ingress Rate Limiting on Internal Campaigns:** There was no internal throttling or concurrency control on bulk campaign dispatches. Marketing payloads were handed directly to `notifier` at maximum burst speed.
- **Initial Alert Ambiguity:** While the dashboard quickly pointed to quota issues, the primary alerting rule alerted broadly on email failure rates rather than distinguishing upstream account quota exhaustion from transport timeouts.
- **Customer Uncertainty:** Support agents experienced an influx of tickets from users wondering whether their salon and clinic slots had actually been booked or paid for, as the checkout success page stated an email confirmation was on its way.

---

## 7. Where We Got Lucky

- **Favourable Quota Window Timing:** The daily quota reset window happened to occur at 19:40 JST, only three hours after the limit was hit. Had the send occurred at 09:00 JST, the delay would have lasted over ten hours, which would have severely degraded trust for same-day salon and health appointments.
- **Queue Memory and Storage Headroom:** The message queue held thousands of backlog items over the three-hour delay without reaching memory thresholds or requiring node autoscaling interventions.

---

## 8. Root Cause Analysis

The root cause was architectural: a failure of isolation between distinct classes of communication traffic.

Sorrel's integration with sendgrid historically relied on a single account configuration and API key. Under this setup, marketing announcements and transactional booking confirmations competed for the exact same daily API credit allocation.

Leading up to the incident, a marketing initiative was prepared to re-engage customers for upcoming autumn bookings. When triggered at 16:15 JST, the marketing dispatch produced thousands of outbound requests in a concentrated burst. At 16:40 JST, this burst consumed the remaining allowance of our daily sendgrid tier.

Once the quota was exhausted, sendgrid's API rejected all further requests across the board. Because `notifier` was shared across both categories, critical one-to-one confirmation receipts could not bypass the block.

When the on-call engineer and platform team reviewed the issue, upgrading the tier immediately on the vendor portal was proposed. However, increasing plan thresholds involves recurring commercial spending commitments. As the service owner, Tom reviewed the metrics and determined that:
1. The queue was stable, non-lossy, and continuing to cycle retries cleanly without dropping payloads.
2. The quota reset was scheduled to take place automatically in three hours.
3. Upgrading the quota would fail to address the systemic flaw, as larger unthrottled marketing sends could simply exhaust higher quotas in the future.

Consequently, the decision was made to allow the queue to drain naturally at reset and resolve the architectural defect via structural separation.

## 10. Operational Lessons

1. **Transactional Egress Must Always Be Isolated:** Operational and transactional notifications (appointment confirmations, payment receipts, password resets) must never share third-party quotas, credentials, or rate limits with bulk marketing campaigns. Commercial marketing traffic must be treated as untrusted, deprioritised load.
2. **Backoff and Queue Durability Prevent Data Loss:** The behaviour of `notifier` during this incident reaffirmed our core operational philosophy: an upstream outage or rate-limiting event should never result in silent failure or dropped customer records. Durable queues with automatic retry mechanisms turned an irreversible operational outage into a transient latency degradation.
3. **Purchasing Decisions Are Not On-Call First Moves:** Financial and tier changes with external providers belong strictly to service owners and management. Resisting the impulse to unilaterally increase external service limits during an incident allowed the team to diagnose the architectural flaw and implement a sound, permanent engineering fix.
