## 3. Notable alert investigations

### 3.1 `web-4xx` alert recurrence (renamed help route)

The only alert rule to trigger multiple times during the shift was `web-4xx`. Under section 14 of the handbook, `web-4xx` triggers when the 4xx HTTP response rate across the service exceeds 1 % for 10 consecutive minutes.

#### First occurrence (2024-03-19 14:22 JST)
- **Symptom:** The 4xx rate on the `Sorrel / web` dashboard nudged up to 1.18 % across nodes in ap-northeast-1.
- **Action taken:** The deploy history in the deploy tool for project "sorrel", service "web", showed the last deploy was `d-3841`, pushed 28 hours prior by Priya. Because the deploy was not recent, rolling back was not considered. I executed the first move `read logs` using the log search tool:
  ```text
  service=web status>=400 route=*
  ```
- **Findings:** The elevated status codes were almost entirely HTTP 404 responses targeting `/help/booking/rescheduling-policy`. A review of recent commits confirmed that our public support articles had been reorganized three days prior, moving this specific documentation article to `/help/appointments/reschedule`. External bookmarks, stale browser tabs, and cached external web links were requesting the previous path. 
- **Customer impact:** None. Booking flows, salon schedules, business calendar dashboards, and checkout sessions were unaffected. The core application routes had a 4xx error rate well below 0.2 %.
- **Severity assignment:** P3. Requests were served normally, and no operational function was broken.

#### Second occurrence (2024-03-21 16:40 JST)
- **Symptom:** The same alert rule fired again during the afternoon peak, reaching 1.09 % for 11 minutes.
- **Action taken:** I checked the `Sorrel / web` dashboard and repeated the log query to verify whether any new route had degraded. The errors remained identical: repeated requests to `/help/booking/rescheduling-policy`. Origin health was pristine, memory and CPU across the six nodes in each region were nominal, and edge metrics on the cloudflare dashboard confirmed no CDN asset failures.
- **Remediation & handoff:** Rather than leaving this route to flap the alert whenever public traffic spikes, I opened a backlog ticket (`WEB-1482`) assigned to Aiko's team. The ticket requests an explicit 301 permanent redirect from `/help/booking/rescheduling-policy` to `/help/appointments/reschedule`. Once deployed in a standard release, this will eliminate the redundant 404s and drop baseline 4xx metrics back below alerting thresholds.

### 3.2 Transient partner rejection on api (2024-03-22 09:15 JST)

On Friday morning, `api-partner-reject` triggered briefly for partner account `ptnr-772`. The alert condition evaluates when a single partner experiences rejections above 50 % over a 15-minute window.
- **Context:** Looking at the "Sorrel / api" board, the partner traffic panel revealed a burst of calendar synchronization calls exceeding their tier limit.

The burn rate on our monthly booking SLO remained unaffected, replica lag was steady at 12 ms, and connection pools across the eight nodes in Tokyo were sitting at 28 % capacity. I logged the event as P3, first move `no action`, and informed Daniel (api engineer) during normal working hours.

## 4. Seasonal traffic trends: March spring peak

Engineers taking the shift this week should be aware of seasonal patterns outlined in section 13.3 of the handbook.

### Spring school-term surge
March is traditionally an intense transition period in Japan. The conclusion of the academic year, graduations, and preparations for the school term starting in early April generate significant demand across specific merchant categories.
- **Tutor bookings:** Bookings for private tutors, test-prep academies, and academic instructors are currently up roughly 30 % compared to February volumes.
- **Other categories:** Hair salons and photography studios are similarly seeing elevated booking densities, whereas medical and physical therapy clinics are operating at standard seasonal baselines.

### Operational implications for `api` and database performance
1. **Monday morning write spikes (09:00–12:00 JST):**
   - Monday mornings represent the heaviest write volume of the week across Sorrel. Business owners open their web calendars, bulk-adjust available slots, confirm pending requests, and reschedule students.
   - With tutor volume elevated by 30 %, expect higher write throughput on api nodes and primary database instances in Tokyo (`ap-northeast`).
   - Keep an eye on the `Sorrel / api` dashboard:
     - **Database pool utilization:** Watch pool exhaustion metrics on the primary database instances. The connection pools across our eight nodes scale automatically between eight and twenty nodes if demand rises, but persistent slow queries can tie up connections.
     - **Replica lag:** Replica lag on read replicas normally sits under 30 ms. Ensure write bursts during slot creation do not drive replica lag up, which can cause read-after-write inconsistencies on booking pages.
     - **Search index freshness:** The slot search index ("find a slot near me") processes significantly higher invalidations when hundreds of recurring tutor lessons are scheduled simultaneously. Verify index freshness stays under 5 seconds.
2. **Evening checkout peaks (18:00–23:00 JST):**
   - Parents and students confirm bookings and pay deposits in the evening. Checkout submission rates will peak between 19:00 and 22:00 JST.
   - Watch the card provider response latency panel on `Sorrel / payments` alongside our internal checkout latency to quickly identify whether any slowdown originates inside our stack or upstream at stripe.
