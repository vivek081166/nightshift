# Postmortem: duplicate appointment reminders after a scheduler restart

Last updated: 2024-09-06  
Owner: Tom (notifier)  
Participants: Tom (notifier owner), Yuki (notifier engineer), Hana (support lead), Kenji Sato (platform lead), Sara (platform engineer)

---

## 1. Summary

On 2024-09-02, during the nightly appointment reminder generation run in `ap-northeast-1`, the notifier scheduler process terminated unexpectedly and restarted mid-batch at 02:40 JST. Because the scheduler tracked progress in ephemeral memory without cursor checkpoints in persistent storage, the newly spawned scheduler process restarted the day's reminder selection query from the beginning. 

As a consequence, 8,412 appointment reminders were queued a second time on the notifier queue. Worker nodes processed both copies, delivering duplicate notifications across both email and SMS channels to end customers before the queue drained. In total, 8,412 customers received duplicate reminders. Support logged 37 customer reports. No appointment reminders were lost, dropped, or corrupted, and booking records in `api` remained unaffected throughout the incident.

The permanent fix was written by Yuki (notifier engineer). It adds a deterministic deduplication key of the format `reminder:<booking id>:<date>` to all outgoing reminder payloads, which notifier checks against a shared 24-hour cache before dispatching to external vendors. The fix was reviewed and shipped on 2024-09-05.

---

## 2. Impact

- **Customer Impact:** 8,412 end customers received two identical reminder messages (via SMS or email) for their scheduled appointments. Support handled 37 customer inquiries and complaints regarding the repeated notifications.
- **Data Integrity:** No data was exposed or altered across tenants. Booking states, schedules, customer details, and audit logs in `api` remained completely accurate.
- **Financial Impact:** Zero transaction loss. Minor increase in external messaging vendor usage for the redundant 8,412 deliveries.
- **Service Availability:** The notifier workers remained operational throughout. Messages continued to be processed and delivered to vendors, though delivery of the redundant batch occupied worker capacity for roughly twenty minutes.

---

## 3. Incident Classification

- **Severity:** P2.
- **Reasoning:** Customer-facing communication degraded through duplicate deliveries, but core booking, payment, and scheduling paths were fully functional. No customer data was exposed or lost, no money moved wrongly, and notifications did not fail to reach recipients. Under handbook section 2, a non-destructive degradation that generates customer confusion without blocking customer workflows qualifies as a P2.

## 5. Technical Root Cause

The notifier service relies on a nightly scheduler node in each active region that queries `api` between 02:00 and 04:00 JST for appointments occurring the following business day. The scheduler converts each appointment into a message on notifier's queue, which worker nodes pick up, render against local templates, and push to external messaging providers (Twilio for SMS, SendGrid for email).

Prior to this incident, the scheduler's architecture had two structural flaws:

1. **Ephemeral Run State:** The scheduler kept its pagination cursor and completed booking identifiers exclusively in local memory. When the process crashed at 02:40 JST, the supervisor restarted it cleanly. The application had no awareness that an active run had already dispatched 8,412 reminder records between 02:00 and 02:39 JST. Upon restart, it executed the daily batch query from the beginning.
2. **Stateless Message IDs:** Each message pushed to the queue had an ephemeral unique UUID assigned at enqueue time. Because the unique message ID was generated during each enqueue operation rather than deterministically derived from the underlying entity, the queue and the downstream worker nodes could not identify that the payload represented an appointment that had already been queued minutes earlier.

The worker nodes processed both batches sequentially. Because both batches contained valid appointment details, the workers rendered templates and handed them off to Twilio and SendGrid. As detailed in handbook section 5.4, messages already handed to outside providers cannot be recalled.

---

## 6. What Went Well

- **No Missed Reminders:** Despite the scheduler crash, the restart ensured all appointments scheduled for the day were queued and sent. Zero customer appointments were missed due to a dropped batch.
- **Worker Stability:** The worker nodes handled the unexpected queue spike (nearly double normal overnight peak) without stalling, resource starvation, or thread lockups. Throughput remained steady.
- **Vendor Reliability:** Neither Twilio nor SendGrid rate-limited or rejected the increased volume; vendor error rates remained flat throughout the burst.
- **Support Alignment:** Hana and the support team were informed before the start of business hours in Japan, enabling consistent customer messaging before tickets escalated.

---

## 7. What Went Badly

- **Lack of Enqueue Deduplication:** The worker nodes had no mechanism to detect duplicate appointment notifications because each message was assigned an arbitrary UUID during generation.
- **Scheduler State Volatility:** The scheduler process did not checkpoint its batch execution in a shared datastore, causing it to blindly restart the entire calendar day upon restart.
- **Delayed Root Cause Confirmation:** Because the crash happened in the middle of the scheduled 02:00–04:00 queue build, the on-call engineer spent precious minutes evaluating whether the volume was simply an abnormally high booking day or an actual anomaly.

---

## 8. Where We Got Lucky

- The scheduler crashed early in the batch run rather than late. Had it crashed near completion, the duplicate volume would have approached 18,000 deliveries, increasing both customer complaints and vendor messaging costs.
- The duplicated messages were identical reminders with correct booking times. Customers were mildly confused, but no incorrect times, corrupted addresses, or wrong service details were distributed.
- The external vendors' daily quotas had sufficient headroom to absorb the extra 8,412 messages without throttling other transactional booking emails or sign-in SMS codes.

---

## 9. Corrective Actions and Fixes

To prevent repeated message generation and delivery following scheduler failures, Yuki (notifier engineer) designed and implemented an application-level deduplication mechanism.

### The Deduplication Architecture

1. **Deterministic Dedupe Keys:**
   Every appointment reminder generated by the scheduler or received via direct service queues must compute a deterministic dedupe key before being placed on the queue or sent downstream. The key format is strictly defined as:
   ```
   reminder:<booking id>:<date>
   ```
   Where `<booking id>` matches the immutable entity ID in `api`, and `<date>` represents the target appointment date formatted as `YYYY-MM-DD`.

2. **24-Hour Deduplication Filter:**
   When a worker node pulls a reminder from the queue, it queries the shared cache for the existence of `reminder:<booking id>:<date>`.
   - If the key does not exist, the worker writes the key with a Time-To-Live (TTL) of 24 hours, renders the message, and proceeds with vendor dispatch.
   - If the key exists, the worker logs `service=notifier msg="duplicate id" dedupe_key=reminder:<booking id>:<date>`, increments the duplicate detection metric, and drops the message immediately without contacting Twilio or SendGrid.

### Implementation and Rollout

- **Author:** Yuki (notifier engineer)
- **Reviewers:** Tom (notifier owner), Sara (platform engineer)
- **Deploy Date:** 2024-09-05 via normal deploy process in the deploy tool.
- **Verification:** Tested in staging with simulated worker restarts and forced multiple enqueues of identical booking IDs. In all test cases, exactly one message was passed to the vendor stub, and subsequent attempts were logged and dropped cleanly.
