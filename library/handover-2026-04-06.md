### 3.2 Alert detail: web-traffic (2026-04-03)

- **Timestamp:** 2026-04-03 14:22 JST
- **Rule:** `web-traffic` (Request volume above 3x the same hour last week for 15 minutes)
- **Severity:** P3
- **First move:** `no action`

#### What happened
At 14:22 JST on Friday afternoon, the `web-traffic` alert fired for `ap-northeast`. Total inbound request volume to `web` spiked to approximately 3.4x the baseline measured at 14:00 JST the previous Friday.

#### Checks conducted
1. **Board review:** Checked the "Sorrel / web" dashboard. The status class breakdown showed a spike concentrated purely in `2xx` and `304` responses. The edge cache hit ratio on cloudflare absorbed roughly 62% of the initial hit, but origin requests rose sufficiently to cross the three-fold threshold.
2. **Customer experience:** Server-side p95 page load remained steady at 240 ms (well below the 2 s threshold). Browser-reported script errors and blank render metrics were completely clean. 5xx rate was 0.01%.
3. **Log inspection:** Ran a targeted search on `sorrel-prod-*` (`service=web status=200 route="/b/*"`). The request stream originated from an automated indexing crawler traversing public merchant landing pages and salon service menus.
4. **api health:** Checked "Sorrel / api" to ensure crawler traffic was not cascading into un-cached database queries. The search index and read replicas showed no elevation in query latency.

#### Disposition
Because requests were being served cleanly, no errors were generated, no internal safety nets were degraded, and zero customers were impacted, this was classified as a P3 (handbook section 2). In line with handbook section 3.5 and section 7.9 (Noisy but harmless traffic), the appropriate first move was `no action`. A line was logged, and a low-priority Jira ticket (`PLAT-4192`) was filed for Aiko to consider refining crawler rate limits on cloudflare during regular working hours.
